# Research Workflow & Source Reliability

> **Part of:** The PetsInMyCity Editorial Operating System (`EDITORIAL_OS.md`, Sections 4–5).
> **Purpose:** The complete investigative workflow every contributor and AI follows, plus the source-reliability tiers used to judge every claim.
> **Companion:** `PUBLISH_GATE.md` (claim statuses & publish gate), `dossiers/README.md` (where this research is recorded).

---

## 1. The workflow (sequential; each step recorded in the dossier)

1. **Frame the questions** a real pet owner in this place would actually ask.
2. **Gather authoritative public data** — geography, climate, demographics, regulations, hazards.
3. **Report local government and agencies** — parks & recreation, animal control/services, county & city records, wildlife/fish-and-game, emergency management, transportation, public health.
4. **Review local publications** — newspapers, TV, established local news sites — for current on-the-ground reality.
5. **Consult domain authorities** — veterinary associations, universities, scientific literature, shelters, rescues.
6. **Study community discussions for leads only** — forums, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor — never as citable fact.
7. **Seek expert voices** — local vets, shelter staff, park officials — on the record where possible.
8. **Verify every meaningful claim** against Section 3 and `PUBLISH_GATE.md`.
9. **Identify what makes this place genuinely different** — the reporting's central discovery.
10. **Only then, write.**

A "meaningful claim" is anything a reader might act on: a rule, hazard, cost, recommendation, location, time, or animal-health fact.

---

## 2. Candidate sources by category

Use these as a research map, not a checklist to skim:
- **Federal:** US Census / American Community Survey, NOAA / National Weather Service, CDC, USDA, National Park Service, EPA (air quality).
- **State:** wildlife / fish-and-game agencies, state health departments, state park systems, departments of agriculture, emergency management.
- **County & city:** parks & recreation, animal control / animal services, code / ordinances, records, county extension offices.
- **Institutional:** universities, veterinary schools & associations (AVMA, board specialty colleges), ASPCA / APCC (toxicology), peer-reviewed journals.
- **Local knowledge:** local newspapers & TV, historical societies, tourism boards (logistics, not promotion), community organizations, event calendars.
- **Businesses / services:** shelters & rescues (their own reported data), Google Places (live business layer), OpenStreetMap (parks, trails, amenities and tags).
- **Leads only:** Reddit, Facebook groups, Nextdoor, TikTok, YouTube, Wikipedia (as a map to its own citations), anonymous reviews.

## 3. Source Reliability Framework (rank relative to the specific claim)

A tier is not a blanket score. A Tier 1 federal source can be the *wrong* authority for a hyperlocal fact, and a local newspaper (Tier 3) may be the *best* source for a neighborhood pattern. Judge each source against the specific claim it supports.

### Tier 1 — Authoritative / primary
Federal agencies (Census, NOAA/NWS, CDC, USDA, NPS, EPA), state agencies (wildlife, health, parks), city & county government (parks, animal control, ordinances, records), accredited universities, peer-reviewed journals, recognized veterinary bodies (AVMA, specialty colleges, ASPCA/APCC).
**Why:** direct authority, accountability, correctability. **Citable as fact once currency is confirmed.** Safety-floor claims require Tier 1–2.

### Tier 2 — Credible institutional
Established veterinary & industry organizations, major shelters/rescues reporting their own data, historical societies, tourism boards (factual logistics), reputable national/regional news with editorial standards.
**Why:** institutional accountability, but a step removed or with some interest. **Citable with attribution; corroborate high-stakes claims.**

### Tier 3 — Useful with corroboration
Local newspapers & TV, community organizations, specialist blogs with identifiable credentialed authors.
**Why:** often the best window into current local reality, but variable rigor and possible local bias. **Use for reporting; corroborate meaningful or safety-relevant claims with a higher tier where one exists.**

### Tier 4 — Leads only, never cited as fact
Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor, TikTok, YouTube, Wikipedia (as source), anonymous reviews, unattributed forums.
**Why:** anonymous, unverifiable, gameable, frequently stale. **Sole legitimate use: surface leads to verify against Tier 1–3.** Wikipedia is a fine map to its own Tier 1–2 citations — follow those. **Nothing from Tier 4 ever appears on a page as established fact.**

## 4. Evaluating a source

Ask of every source, for every claim:
- **Authority:** does it have direct authority over *this* fact?
- **Currency:** is it current enough that the fact is still true today?
- **Independence:** is it free of a commercial or promotional motive to distort?
- **Verifiability:** can another researcher check it?

A source is trustworthy for a claim when authority, currency, and verifiability all hold. **Reject** a claim whose only support is anonymous, uncorroborated, commercially motivated, or unverifiable — no matter how good it would make the page look.

---

## 5. Handling conflicting information

- Prefer the **higher tier** and the **more current** source.
- When credible sources of similar authority genuinely disagree, **report the disagreement** rather than choosing silently.
- When an official source conflicts with strong on-the-ground reporting (the ordinance says one thing; every local source says enforcement differs), **report both** and label them — the law versus the practice.
- **Never** resolve a conflict by inventing a tidy answer.

---

## 6. Recording the research

Everything above is logged in the page's dossier (`dossiers/README.md`): sources consulted with tier and URL, a verification entry per meaningful claim with status and date, findings, rejected findings, open questions, and confidence. The dossier — not the article — is the source of truth and our defense if a claim is ever challenged.
